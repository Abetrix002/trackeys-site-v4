Distribution

Why secondary sales visibility is the FMCG blind spot costing you 20% of revenue.

Primary sales leave your warehouse, and visibility drops off a cliff. Here's how modern distributors are closing the gap from plant to shelf, and what it unlocks.

IS
Imran ShaikhHead of Distribution, Trackeys
Apr 14, 2026 · 8 min read

For most FMCG brands, the moment a truck leaves the warehouse is the moment visibility dies. Primary sales are clean, tracked and auditable. Secondary sales (what the distributor actually sells to retail) is a monthly data dump at best, a guess at worst.

The visibility cliff

We recently audited the distribution data of six mid-market FMCG brands across South Asia and the Gulf. On average, they could answer "what did we ship?" within 2 hours, but "what did our distributors sell?" took 3 weeks. Three weeks of blind spots is three weeks of stock rotting, three weeks of promotions going unreconciled, three weeks of competitors moving faster.

The 20% you're leaving on the table

When you don't see secondary sales in real time, four things go wrong: (1) trade spend gets misallocated against the wrong SKUs, (2) replenishment lags demand and you OOS in fast-moving stores, (3) slow movers hide inside aggregate distributor reports, (4) channel P&L becomes a story instead of a number. Together these account for roughly 15–25% of addressable revenue in most of the brands we've analyzed.

The moment we got secondary-sales visibility down to a day, our promo ROI calculation stopped being theoretical. It became something we acted on, weekly.

What closing the gap looks like

Distributors log secondary sales through a portal or mobile app. Van sales direct to trade is captured at source. Both feed one secondary-sales view with SKU-level traceability. Forecast engines then recalibrate against the new signal, which lifts fill rate, cuts obsolescence, and makes trade spend a knob you can actually turn.

It isn't a tech problem anymore. It's a process problem. Most of the distributors we work with had never been asked for daily data. Once asked, and given a tool that made it 30 seconds of work, they provided it happily.

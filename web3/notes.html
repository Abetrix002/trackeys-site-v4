Visual overhaul of trackeys.com

This version keeps the live site's words and page structure exactly: same pages, same section order, same headlines, cards, FAQs, prices, posts and case studies, crawled on 25–26 Oct 2026. Only the presentation is new. The copy check in the build fails if any headline, FAQ answer, post paragraph or case-study line is missing or altered.

V4 · the product's own UI language

V4 takes the Trackeys dashboard (pav.trackeys.com, reviewed 29 Oct 2026 across all 36 menu pages) and uses it wherever the site shows the product. The marketing site and the app now look like one company.

Data. The live dashboard holds real client records (shop owners, phone numbers, addresses). None of it is used. Every name, code, district assignment and figure in the windows is sample data. District names are public Jeddah neighbourhoods. Turn on review notes to see this flagged on each window.

Not used. Master data, users and roles, approval workflows, webhooks, subscriptions, payment histories, activity logs and settings: these are admin tables with nothing to show a buyer. Reports is a three-card launcher with emoji icons, so it adds nothing visually.

Maps. Every map is now a real Saudi street map built from OpenStreetMap data (downloaded 29 Oct 2026): Riyadh around Al Olaya, King Fahd Road and Al Uruba Road for the home, live, routing, rep and network scenes and the Route Plans window, and Jeddah with its real district boundaries (Al Rawdah, Al Salamah, Al Zahra and neighbours) for Assign Districts. Each van route is computed along real roads between its stops, and the re-route on the home page detours around the blocked stretch. Streets, buildings and parks come as a light and a dark image that swap with the theme. Street and district names are drawn in the site font. Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL); the credit sits on each map. The generator is src3/map/render.py.

Copy vs. map. The live site's copy around the maps names Dubai places (lat 25.197 · lng 55.274, DXB route codes, Al Barsha); amounts are shown in SAR. The copy is kept verbatim, so it now sits on a Riyadh map. Either localise the copy or pick a Dubai map before launch.

Anti-slop pass (29 Oct). An Impeccable critique (independent design review plus detector, 17/32) found the site generic below the hero. Fixed without touching copy or section order:

  • Cards and stats: capability cards are now typographic spec blocks, not keyword-picked filler art. Hero stats are a quiet row, and the outcomes band is the one big-number moment.
  • Labels and tiles: eyebrows are plain labels instead of chips. Icon tiles are gone from roles, add-ons and case studies, and integrations are names instead of monograms.
  • Motion: "How it works" is a static sequence, not a scroll-jacked pin. The tilt reveal, magnetic buttons, marquees and pulsing dots are gone.
  • Decoration: button glows, radial halos, hero grid and the CTA swoosh are replaced by the product's own navy street-grid squares, and the logo now sits on its navy tile.
  • Fixes: the mobile menu opens below the header and gains a close icon. Heading order, label sizes, the disabled button, the visible password label and the blog avatars are corrected.

Detector hits went from 669 to about 330. The ones left are intentional: the brand grid motif, the Copilot thinking dots, the dashboard mockups (card-in-card, small labels), the week labels on the pricing timeline, and em-dashes in live-site copy.

Integration logos. Each stack is now an always-scrolling strip of the real tool logos (requested 29 Oct; it reverses the earlier no-marquee call for this one section). Logos sit on white tiles so brand colours hold in both themes, sized to equal visual area, with the one-line description underneath. The tool name is carried by the logo and its alt text. The strip pauses on hover, and with reduced motion it becomes a static grid. Sources: Wikimedia Commons, Simple Icons (Shell) and the vendors' own websites (Samsara, Geotab, Circana, Blue Yonder, Fleetio, Motive, Manhattan, WEX). They are trademarks of their owners; showing them implies an integration, so confirm each connector exists, or get partner approval, before launch.

Small screens. Each window is drawn at 1,200 px and scaled to fit like a screenshot, so it stays faithful to the product rather than reflowing into something the app never shows.

Flags kept for review

Turn on Show review notes in the dark bar. It marks content that comes from the live site but can't be verified (customer logos, case studies and named quotes, unsourced percentages, blog authors, the integration list, prices and trial terms), plus places where the live site has no destination (Terms, Cookies and Fleet Management in the footer). The "Watch 2-min overview" and "Download datasheet" buttons were removed on 1 Nov 2026, and prices switch between USD, SAR and EUR (SAR at the 3.75 peg). Integrations, Documentation and Security were removed from the footer, and the homepage "How the model works" button was removed, on 1 Nov 2026. The live Vercel site omits this prototype bar, the review notes and this page. Two deliberate changes: the footer's Privacy link now opens the existing policy page (the live site links it to #), and the demo form's step 2 (calendar) and confirmation use minimal new copy, because the live step 2 can't be seen without sending a real request.
